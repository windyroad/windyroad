### First-encounter Comprehension

For a cold read, use a fresh reviewer with no conversation, author rationale or previous verdicts. Read in order, in short sections. At each figure, inspect its visible title, labels, legend and caption before continuing. Include text alternatives in their own reading order; an unseen image description does not explain a visible label to a sighted reader. If you cannot inspect a figure, report that coverage gap rather than passing it.

Judge meaning using only what a reader has encountered so far and the intended audience's ordinary knowledge. Record a comprehension finding before moving on. A definition later in the article may explain the author's intent, but does not erase the earlier interruption. Do not borrow context from implementation files, review receipts or your own plausible technical explanation.

Familiar words can form an opaque label. Ask what concrete thing the phrase refers to, what action or difference it describes, and whether the reader needs that distinction here. Flag labels that require guessing those answers, including compressed implementation names in otherwise readable charts. Quote the wording, state what is missing at that point, and suggest a proportionate fix: introduce the meaning nearby, use an already-understood comparison, or move the detail to where it is explained.

Do not demand definitions for ordinary audience vocabulary or details unnecessary to the current claim. Distinguish a comprehension obstacle from a style preference. Report strengths and actionable weaknesses; an unresolved obstacle prevents a clean comprehension pass. Keep an excerpt review scoped to the excerpt. These are COGA findings, not automatic WCAG failures.
