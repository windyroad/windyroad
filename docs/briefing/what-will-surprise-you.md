## What Will Surprise You

> Older entries rotate to [`what-will-surprise-you-archive.md`](what-will-surprise-you-archive.md) under the Tier 3 budget; nothing has ever been deleted. Rotation here is by signal rather than age. The archive tail is deliberately NOT split further even though it is past the 2x must-split ratio: nothing loads an archive at session start, so another generation behind the `-archive-early` siblings would be churn. That decision and its measurements are recorded on P150; do not re-litigate it from the byte counts alone. Most recent pass: 2026-08-23 (P145 iter).

- **Two reviewers converging independently is the strongest signal this stack produces, and it means stop, not iterate.** On 2026-08-23 the architect and the JTBD reviewer, given different prompts and reading different corpora, independently killed the same drafted `.github/dependabot.yml`: the architect on the ground that it reinstated a bot authoring path whose acceptance died with a supersession, the JTBD reviewer on the ground that it routed a code-quality judgement to a persona whose file explicitly excludes it. Neither had seen the other's verdict. When that happens the answer is not a better version of the artefact, it is that the artefact is the wrong shape. Dropping it and recording the open question took one round; arguing with either reviewer alone would have taken several and landed the same place. Third observation of this pattern, after the 2026-08-09 P109 iteration and the 2026-08-23 P141 iteration. **A fourth on 2026-08-23 extends it to three reviewers and to a case where the artefact was right and one line of it was wrong**: on the P154 fix the architect, the JTBD reviewer and the voice reviewer each independently found that a new lint check stripped every parenthesised span while its own header claimed it stripped only link URLs. None had seen the others. So convergence does not always mean stop; it means the finding is real and the only question left is which surface moves. There the code moved to match the header, because the header was describing the behaviour anyone would want.

- **A `--body-file` call cannot clear the external-comms gate by reviewing the body, and the fix is to review NOTHING.** The gate scrapes the draft out of the command string: heredoc, then `--body "..."`, then `-m "..."`. With `--body-file` nothing matches, so it hashes an EMPTY draft, `sha256("" + "\n" + surface)`, while the reviewer hashes whatever you put in `<draft>`. The keys never meet and the gate re-blocks after a genuine PASS, telling you to run the review you just ran. This is recorded in the archive from 2026-08-08 and was re-hit on 2026-08-29 filing agent-plugins#453, costing three wasted reviewer dispatches, because the archive is not loaded at session start. The archived fix was to inline the body; for anything past a few hundred bytes that is miserable. The cheaper fix, confirmed working: keep `--body-file`, and dispatch each reviewer with an **empty** `<draft></draft>` block plus a pointer to the file, so the key it produces matches the empty draft the gate hashed. Two extra mechanics make it land. The risk reviewer has no Bash tool and cannot compute a sha256, so hand it the value: `printf '%s\n%s' "" "gh-issue-create" | shasum -a 256 | cut -d' ' -f1`. And the voice-tone reviewer derives its key from your prompt rather than from an emitted line, so its prompt needs the same `SURFACE:` line and the same empty `<draft>` block. Both must be SYNCHRONOUS `Agent` spawns or their mark hooks never fire.
