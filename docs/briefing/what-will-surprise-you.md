## What Will Surprise You

> Older entries rotated to [`what-will-surprise-you-archive.md`](what-will-surprise-you-archive.md) on 2026-08-08, three times on 2026-08-09, and again on 2026-08-23 (Tier 3 budget). Nothing has ever been deleted. The 2026-08-23 pass moved the commit-message external-comms gate entry and the CI smoke-test entry, and added two from the P141 iteration. Rotation here is by signal rather than age, and the archive tail is deliberately NOT split further even though it is past the 2x must-split ratio: nothing loads an archive at session start, so another generation behind the `-archive-early` siblings would be churn. That decision and its measurements are recorded on P150; do not re-litigate it from the byte counts alone.

- **Every governance marker in this stack is written by a `PostToolUse` hook whose matcher is the literal string `Agent`, so anything that is not a fresh synchronous `Agent` spawn marks nothing.** Six hooks, one shape: architect, jtbd, risk-score, voice-tone, external-comms and style-guide. A `SendMessage` resume fires none of them, and neither does a background-launched reviewer. Only `wr-architect` documents this, in one deny string (`hooks/lib/architect-gate.sh:64`, the P400 note); the external-comms gate documents the background half only (`external-comms-gate.sh:413`, P402). The other four say nothing, so the block arrives with no way to work out why. Two corollaries worth holding. Widening the matcher would not fix it as the hooks are written, because they identify the reviewer from `tool_input.subagent_type` and a `SendMessage` payload has no such field. And on `wr-architect` 0.20.0, which is what this repo runs, verdict FORMATTING is not a cause: the mark hook routes an unparseable verdict into the same allow-with-marker branch as PASS, so a block after a genuine PASS means the marker landed under a session ID nobody reads (P088), not that the heading was the wrong shape. Recorded on P124. **The uniformity is version-pinned and is already breaking upstream**: `wr-risk-scorer` 0.18.6, sitting in the cache for another project, has collapsed its four hooks into one dispatcher whose `PostToolUse` matcher is `Agent|Bash|Edit|Skill|Write|` plus eleven agent-lifecycle events (`spawn_agent`, `wait_agent`, `close_agent` and their `multi_agent_v1__` forms). Two things follow. Re-read the hooks after any plugin upgrade rather than trusting this entry. And there is upstream precedent for widening the matcher, which strengthens the P124 fix recommendation, though note that even 0.18.6's list does not include `SendMessage`, so the resume gap plausibly survives it.

- **A governance subagent's "X does not exist" is a bounded-search result, and it is wrong often enough to check.** On 2026-08-23 the architect reported `wr-architect-generate-decisions-compendium` "not present on this box" and told the orchestrator not to assume it existed. It globbed the repo; the binary is on `$PATH`. The verdict was otherwise excellent, which is exactly what makes this dangerous: an absence claim arrives wrapped in findings you have already decided to trust. This is the same family as the ticket-prose and Fix-Strategy cases, now observed on a third surface, and the check is one `command -v` away. Correct it back to the subagent in the next round: it accepted the correction immediately and the rest of the review improved.

- **Two reviewers converging independently is the strongest signal this stack produces, and it means stop, not iterate.** On 2026-08-23 the architect and the JTBD reviewer, given different prompts and reading different corpora, independently killed the same drafted `.github/dependabot.yml`: the architect on the ground that it reinstated a bot authoring path whose acceptance died with a supersession, the JTBD reviewer on the ground that it routed a code-quality judgement to a persona whose file explicitly excludes it. Neither had seen the other's verdict. When that happens the answer is not a better version of the artefact, it is that the artefact is the wrong shape. Dropping it and recording the open question took one round; arguing with either reviewer alone would have taken several and landed the same place. Second observation of this pattern after the 2026-08-09 P109 iteration.
