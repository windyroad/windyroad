## Newsletter pipeline: why external review is still needed (archive)

Entries rotated out of [the live topic file](./newsletter-pipeline-why-external-review-is-still-needed-2026.md) on 2026-08-23 under the ADR-040 Tier 3 budget. Rotated because they were not exercised in the sessions since they were written, not because they were judged wrong.

- **Detection was never the bottleneck, and Issue 17 settled it with numbers.** On 2026-08-10 all ten gates were run against a single frozen digest instead of against moving text. They returned roughly thirty findings and caught every substantive factual, sourcing and claim-calibration defect in the edition, including four that a human external reviewer missed. In the same session six of that reviewer's own factual assertions about the artefacts did not survive checking. Stop reading external review as evidence that the gates are weak. The failure mode was that the gates were advisory, or stale, or skipped, not that they cannot see. <!-- signal-score: -2 | last-classified: 2026-08-23 | first-written: 2026-08-07 -->

- **Remediation is where the defects come from.** Successive Issue 17 rounds recorded two of three blockers as fix-induced. Splitting a long sentence into a list swallowed the next paragraph into a bullet. A rewrite left a paragraph ending duplicated. A fix for an over-claim produced an inverted cross-reference. Re-read the whole passage after every fix, not the line you changed, and expect the second gate sweep to find things the first did not because you put them there. <!-- signal-score: -2 | last-classified: 2026-08-23 | first-written: 2026-08-07 -->

- **Read the PRIOR EDITION's published artefacts before drafting, not just the skill template.** Recurring conventions live only in the corpus. Issue 15's LinkedIn post is 1,655 chars and carries an issue line ("Issue 15 of The Shift: ..."), the label "This week:", and a closing reply instruction. Issue 16's draft had none of the three and reached 2,993 chars, hitting LinkedIn's 3,000 ceiling three times; each time space was bought by cutting something load-bearing, and one of those cuts came back two rounds later as a reviewer finding. The template encodes the shape in the abstract; the last edition encodes what it actually looks like. <!-- signal-score: -2 | last-classified: 2026-08-23 | first-written: 2026-08-07 -->

- **The LinkedIn companion is a teaser, not a compressed article.** The failure mode is packing the brief's argument into the post. Tom's correction: "the post is too long. The guts are in the article." Cutting the Martin/Booch/compilers passage to a single pointer sentence took it from 2,993 to 2,364 chars and made room for the four elements that were missing. <!-- signal-score: -2 | last-classified: 2026-08-23 | first-written: 2026-08-07 -->

- **A regression test that passes against the buggy code proves nothing, and this is easy to get wrong.** The first P119 test passed pre-fix. Two fixture defects: padding under the 64KB pipe buffer (so SIGPIPE could never fire) and padding placed BEFORE the matched heading (so little was left unwritten when grep exited). Always run the falsification in BOTH directions: revert the fix, confirm the test fails, restore, confirm it passes. <!-- signal-score: -2 | last-classified: 2026-08-23 | first-written: 2026-08-07 -->
