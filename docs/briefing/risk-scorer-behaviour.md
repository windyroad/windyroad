- **The risk scorer is a design reviewer, not a formality.** On 2026-08-08 it stopped a commit twice at 12/25 and both stops caught genuine design defects the architect passes had missed: a boolean comparison where the design specified four states (which would have fired on every prep-carried verdict, every Monday), and a fix that had only been half-applied because a regex missed indented template slots. Later the same day it stopped the P126 fix twice more, at 6/25: once on an ownership asymmetry (the rollback branch honoured the operator's in-flight manifest edits while the commit branch would have swept them into a `chore(deps)` commit), once on push:watch committing a lockfile regeneration nothing had executed. Five architect rounds had passed the first of those. Treat a STOP as a finding to read, not a gate to satisfy.

- **The scorer will catch a false claim in the fix's own write-up, including one in a paragraph arguing for carefulness.** On 2026-08-23 the P151 Resolution said the new check fires on "the two published editions on disk". Two were the witnesses the ticket named and the only two that had been run; sixteen editions carry a reviews sibling and all sixteen fire. The sentence had already been copied into the README's verification watch-line, so the next verifier would have re-linted the archive, seen sixteen, and had to work out whether the check had regressed. Nothing in a test or a hook can catch a wrong prose claim about corpus state; the scorer read the corpus. Ask it to verify claims against disk on every dispatch, and when it says a figure is wrong, go and count.

- **Score a fix by what a failure of the fix would cost, not by what the original defect cost.** The same 2026-08-23 pass first put a residual at 8/25, above the appetite of 5, and re-scored it to 4 on the second pass after naming its own error: it had charged the commit with the impact of the defect the commit was fixing. The distinction is worth carrying into how you read any residual on a fix. It also declined to credit the thing that looked like a remediation: recording a residual in the ticket and queueing it for the maintainer earned zero reduction, because deferral is not a control. Both corrections came from the scorer, unprompted, on a re-score it was asked to run because the first verdict was a STOP.

- **The scorer catches the same defect class twice in a row, including once inside the round written to fix it, so a correction pass is not a safe place to relax.** On 2026-08-29 a P176 capture was scored at 6/25, above the appetite of 5, because its Symptoms section stated commands as observed that had never been run: the ticket named `npm run eval:newsletter` when the observation came from a locally installed promptfoo against a throwaway config, and asserted a causal link its own investigation task called inference. The remediation round fixed all five findings and introduced two fresh ones of the same class, which the re-score caught: it said `falsify.sh`'s guard fires on a dirty tree when the guard is `git diff --quiet -- "$SKILL_PATH"` over a single file, and it counted five CI `setup-node` steps where disk shows six, from a grep whose own output had printed six. The third score came in at 4 and still flagged a sufficiency claim resting on an unstated precondition. Two things generalise. Ask it to verify the artefact's claims against disk and it will read the scripts, the workflows and the object database, not just the diff. And the specific hazard is that a correction round feels like the careful pass, which is exactly when a transcription error rides through unchecked.
