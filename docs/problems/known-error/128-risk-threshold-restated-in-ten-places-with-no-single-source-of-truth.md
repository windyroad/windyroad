## Root cause

Two facts are conflated in one number. `RISK-POLICY.md` is the source of truth for the **operative** threshold, because the commit hook parses it. ADR-007 and ADR-008 are the source of truth for **why the threshold was what it was at that date**. Those are different facts that do not need collapsing, but nothing pointed between them until 2026-08-07, so every restatement read as current.

## Symptoms

An agent doing compliance review reads `< 5` from the compendium's in-force section and treats it as the rule, potentially flagging the amended policy as non-compliant or attempting to revert it.

## Workaround

`RISK-POLICY.md` is the enforcement surface. Where it and an ADR disagree on the threshold, the policy wins. The dated pointers added in `fea3cec` say so at both surfaces.

## Impact Assessment

- **Who is affected**: the architect agent on routine compliance review, and any agent or reader reasoning about the threshold from the decision record.
- **Frequency**: every compliance review that consults ADR-007 or the compendium's in-force section.
- **Severity**: no gate verdict can be wrong from this, since the hook never reads the ADRs. The cost is misdirected reasoning and rework.
- **Analytics**: none.

**ADR-054 settles the instrument, and it rules out the one this ticket has been using.** `docs/decisions/054-a-decision-is-changed-by-a-new-decision-never-by-editing-the-old-one.proposed.md` was recorded on 2026-08-16, a week after the last work here, and decides what Investigation Task 1 was asking. A decision is changed by a new decision; the overtaken record is never edited except in its frontmatter. So the fix is a **new decision record** carrying the current threshold, with `amends:` naming each overtaken clause specifically, and a reciprocal `amended-by:` on each of the three carriers.

**The two directions are not symmetric, and reading them as symmetric makes the two paragraphs below look contradictory.** Adding a **forward** `amends:` claim to a record that is already ratified is the act ADR-054 forbids; it says so of ADR-052 in its own words. Adding a **reciprocal** `amended-by:` to a ratified record that has been overtaken is the opposite, and is required: ADR-054 records thirteen frontmatter lines across thirteen files doing exactly that.

**The dated pointers this ticket has been relying on are the forbidden act, done before the rule existed.** ADR-007, ADR-008 and ADR-027 are all `human-oversight: confirmed` as of 2026-05-30, and the pointers at their lines 15, 16 and 16 are edits to the bodies of ratified records. Leave them: removing one is a second body edit to undo a first. Do not add a fourth, and in particular do not put inline staleness markers beside the ten stale strings, which was the obvious next move and is now closed off. State their status precisely, though. They are **not** covered by the grandfathering ADR-054 gives the six `## Amendment` sections it names, which are ADR-019, ADR-020, ADR-026, ADR-032, ADR-042 and ADR-043, hard-coded as `AMENDMENT_ALLOWLIST` in `scripts/decisions-supersession.test.mjs`. These three are blockquotes rather than `## Amendment` headings, so the test neither catches them nor protects them. They sit outside the allowlist, not inside it.

**ADR-049 cannot be extended to carry this.** It uses `related:` and no `amends:`, so the band move it records is not in the relationship graph at all, and it is ratified, so adding a forward claim to it is forbidden. Same reasoning ADR-054 applies to itself when it declines to add ADR-018 to ADR-052's list.

**The one shape that would land cleanly today is a trap, so close it off.** The threshold record could be authored with `related:` only and no `amends:`, exactly as ADR-049 was. That stays green, and a brand-new file fires the compendium hook only to insert its own entry, so ADR-027's annotation is never re-derived. It is safe to land unattended, and it is still wrong. Once that record is ratified, adding a forward `amends:` to it is forbidden by the asymmetry above. So it buys a green test today and either strands the threshold move outside the relationship graph permanently or forces a fourth record later to carry the claim. Holding costs nothing by comparison.

**The threshold record is wanted whatever happens to ADR-054, and that half is not blocked.** The 2026-08-07 appetite move is a shipped decision governing every commit, push and release, and it has no record anywhere in `docs/decisions/`. `RISK-POLICY.md` line 36 and its Amendment History are the whole of it. ADR-049 declines it in terms: "The bar must not move. Tom asked for the band, not the threshold." That record would be wanted if ADR-054 had never been written and will still be wanted if ADR-054 is rejected. Nor is the vocabulary contingent: `amends:`/`amended-by:` is live across 21 files and 24 keys, fourteen records carry the reciprocal, and all of it predates 2026-08-16. Do not treat the whole fix as an application of ADR-054. Only the graph-wiring half is.

**What blocks it is the wiring, and the block is sharper than a ratification.** Two things on disk mean the wiring cannot be landed unattended:

1. `scripts/decisions-supersession.test.mjs` asserts reciprocity in **both** directions. A forward `amends:` whose target carries no `amended-by:` goes red, and a lone `amended-by:` whose target names nothing under `amends:` goes red too. So there is no split at all: you cannot author the record with `amends:` and hold the three reciprocals, and you cannot pre-place the three reciprocals and add `amends:` later. The halves land together or not at all.
2. Writing frontmatter to ADR-007, ADR-008 and ADR-027 is a write to each file, which fires `architect-compendium-update-entry.sh` and re-derives all three compendium entries wholesale from their current bodies. The hook does not edit the entry: it spawns a model call instructed to re-author the entry **from the record's current body**, passing the existing entry as context with no instruction to preserve it. Investigation Task 5 above already recorded that this rewrite drops hand annotations, having eaten the two `fea3cec` annotations once. Its fail-closed guard does not help here, because it compares only the set of ADR ids and the count of headings, and is blind to content changes inside the entry it edited. On success the hook stages `docs/decisions/README.md` itself, so a lost annotation is staged silently rather than left where `git status` would show it. The ADR-027 entry is exactly such an annotation: it is the single `< 5` left in the read-first surface and is safe only because a dated clause names it stale, while ADR-027's body still carries bare `(< 5)` at lines 65 and 94. A re-derivation that picks those up without the pointer reintroduces an unmarked stale figure into the surface this ticket's Likelihood was rated on, while closing the record gap. Whoever lands this must diff `docs/decisions/README.md` after the frontmatter edits and before committing, and hand-correct if the annotation is gone. That diff is not an extra precaution; it is the only control that exists.

That second one is why nothing was authored on 2026-08-23 with the user away. The judgement-bearing half is unblocked, but landing it requires writing to three ratified records behind a hook that has destroyed the relevant annotation before, on the exact surface this ticket exists to protect. Doing that unattended risks re-creating the symptom while claiming to close it.

**And the fix will not reach that surface anyway.** `docs/decisions/README.md` renders `Supersedes:` three times and `amended-by` zero times, while fourteen records carry `amended-by` in frontmatter. That is a renderer gap with a populated input, not an absence. The live renderer is the `architect-compendium-update-entry.sh` hook. ADR-054's reassessment criteria point at the generator generically without naming a script; the full-file one Investigation Task 5 examined is self-declared deprecated. Both are upstream and out of reach here. So a correct fix lands the record and leaves the architect's read-first surface showing no redirect. Whoever re-rates this should account for that rather than assume Likelihood falls to the floor.
