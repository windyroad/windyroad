## Skeptic Review

Round 1's sharpest objection was structural: every one of sixteen items was bent back to confirm the standing thesis, and the brief exempted Anthropic's proposal from the vendor scepticism it had just told readers to apply. Item 3 was rewritten to turn that scepticism on the proposal explicitly.

**Round 2 findings that remain open and are the author's call:**

1. **The scorecard's unit of analysis shifts between entries.** For GCC the "system" is the contribution pipeline; for Anthropic's proposal and the kill-switch bill the "system" is the model itself. Fix the unit consistently and the tally moves. Partially addressed by adding an explicit unit definition to the opener, but the skeptic's point stands that pre-release testing of a shipped model is arguably an output rule under GCC's unit.
2. **The five are selected, not enumerated.** The OAIC guidance binds today and scores as a system rule but sits in Also worth noting; the Microsoft letter and the Alliance are covered at length in Item 3 but are absent from the scorecard. Partially addressed by stating the selection rule in the opener.
3. **Promise-payoff on the opener.** Softened from "what separates a rule that will hold" to "which ones are worth copying", but the edition still concedes that the rules with the right shape are the ones nobody has to follow, which cuts against the frame.
4. **Human angle on Item 1** restates the analysis rather than landing an emotional beat, and abandons the volunteer-maintainer thread it opens.

## Cross-Edition Consistency

**Terminal verdict: SUPPORTED** (round 2). Window: 8 prior editions (2026-06-08 through 2026-07-27).

Round 1 returned **CONTRADICTS** on one finding, and it was a genuine catch. Item 1's original "That is the arrangement inverted" passage asserted that review is human territory which GCC wrongly handed to the machine. That contradicts the series thesis stated in Issues 10, 11, 14 and 15, which argues the opposite: machine review is correct, and Issue 14 states as a positive finding that AI review catches more than human review, with the only condition being that the reviewing agent must differ from the authoring agent. The gate correctly identified that the defensible complaint was only the second half (GCC reserving the writing for humans) and that the fix was a two-sentence rewrite rather than a thesis change. Resolved via the Rewrite path.

Round 2 returned SUPPORTED, confirming no residue of the inversion survives and that the Chrome note carries the same thesis in its strongest form.
