## Critic Review: Newsletter (finalise)

**Terminal verdict: REJECTED, `REJECTED_REASON: critic-loop-exhausted` (round 3, the ADR 016 cap).**

This is the honest record. The three-round budget was spent as follows.

**Round 1** returned WEAKNESSES_FOUND on 13 findings. The most consequential: the system/output test was justified on where humans sit but adjudicated on upstreamness, so the winner did not deliver the payoff the test was sold on; the closing line contradicted the opener's own tally; GCC was tallied as a clean output rule while the body described it as half system-shaped; and the H1's "both halves" was never defined.

**Round 2** returned WEAKNESSES_FOUND on 5. The scorecard's unit of analysis shifted between entries, "the arrangement" was used with three incompatible divisions of labour, and the opener's universal claim about output rules was falsified by the edition's own Item 2.

**Author response between rounds 2 and 3 was structural, not another patch.** The six-entry numbered scorecard and the "exactly one clears both halves" payoff were removed entirely, on the grounds that the device had produced a new coherence failure in every review round across both phases (a 4-vs-5 count error, a "most" that was 1-of-4, a "most" that was 3-of-6, a unit definition that graded the two hinge entries backwards if applied literally, three different inclusion tests for the denominator, and a grading unit that switched between whole-document and best-clause). The decision was escalated to Tom on publish morning with four options and he selected "drop the scorecard".

**Round 3** returned REJECTED on 3 findings, of which the critic itself distinguished one as materially different in kind:

1. Two dangling references to the removed scorecard survived in body copy ("On the scorecard that is a system rule", "why it scores as a system rule"). The critic noted this had "no editorial defence available". **Fixed after the verdict.**
2. The opener's stated cost of an output rule was not evidenced by the items it foregrounds. **Fixed after the verdict** (the opener now names GCC as the instance).
3. Item 4 sat outside the opener's own question and was never placed back on it. **Fixed after the verdict** (Item 4's why-it-matters now opens by placing training data on the system side).

All three round-3 findings were remediated after the terminal verdict was issued. The critic budget for this artifact pass was exhausted at that point, so the fixes are unverified by the critic. Voice and content-risk both re-ran on the remediated text and both PASS.

**Author decision required.** The edition carries a terminal REJECTED from the newsletter critic. Per the skill's failure-mode table this is a publish-or-rewrite decision for Tom, not an automatic block.

## Author correction to the thesis (post-gate, publish morning)

Tom corrected the edition's central position mid-run, after the gates had settled. The draft had argued that GCC's arrangement was "upside down", implying machines should write and humans should review. His actual position is stronger:

> with the right setup, AI can (and should) do both the authoring and the review. The author of clean code agree - he no longer reviews AI generated code. THe correct setup is "Human on the Loop" where humans review the AI review process - get humans to ratify the ADRs, Personas, Jobs to Be Done, Risk controls, etc.

The From Tom opener, Item 1 and the closing CTA were rewritten around this. Robert Martin's July position was verified and quoted verbatim from his own post, and Grady Booch's dissent was added as the honest counterweight. This aligns the edition with the codified P101 editorial stance in `three-lens-filter.md`, which the earlier framing had drifted off.

Tom also corrected Item 4's human angle, which asserted the training-set certification consequence universally. It is now gated behind an explicit scope question ("has your organisation trained or fine-tuned its own models?") with both branches stated.

Voice and content-risk both re-ran after each correction. Voice FAILed twice on the new prose (a reintroduced "actually"; unglossed framework terms in the Grade 9-10 opener) and PASSed on the third pass. Content-risk PASSed.

## Skeptic Review (brief, finalise)

**Terminal verdict: WEAKNESSES_FOUND** (round 2). Non-blocking save-but-revise per ADR-042.

Round 1's twelve findings were substantially addressed, including several genuine accuracy failures: an unsupported "four incidents" count, a "fair use" defence mischaracterised in a German court, a mistranslated court name, an unsourced causal claim about why Anthropic ran its review, and a "first major European ruling" claim contradicted by the brief's own primary source (whose title says "second").

Round 2's findings largely dissolved with the scorecard's removal. The residual objection that survives and is **not** resolved:

> The test is unfalsifiable as posed, because the framing does the work. Everything the piece likes ends up on the system side; the only rule sorted onto the output side is GCC's, the one item the piece set out to disagree with.

The LinkedIn post adopted the skeptic's own proposed remedy (pin the test to something checkable: does complying require a human to touch every artefact?) and the brief's opener was subsequently rewritten to lead with that same checkable form. The objection is therefore weaker against the final text than against the text it was raised on, but it has not been re-tested, because the skeptic's budget for the brief was spent before the final rewrite.
