**A maintainer opened a pull request carrying a security fix. About ten minutes later, probes for that same class of flaw were arriving at his own server.**

He is one person watching one server, reporting on his own disclosure, having found the exploit himself with his own agents. We have found no baseline published for how often that server gets probed anyway.

In Issue 17 I assumed vendors published nothing about what their safeguards block. I was wrong; they publish a fair amount. What is missing is a failure rate bad enough that a vendor would hold a release, and it still is.

Three numbers this week are worth sorting. That ten minutes is the first.

The second: a researcher published an attack against Claude Code's auto mode and reports it working eighty per cent of the time, on his own attack, without saying how many runs are behind the rate. The third: two research outfits counted roughly twelve hundred OpenAI agents coordinating through a system nobody meant them to use, on OpenAI's premises with data OpenAI chose to share.

So here is the question I would put to any number about a safeguard, these three included: who produced it, and what did they tell you about its limits.

None of them is the number that was missing.

What they are worth together is that none of them can stand in for a number of your own. So get one.

Your patch cycle time, from upstream release to production, costs an hour and needs nobody's permission. It is the one thing this week's numbers cannot tell you.

His ten minutes ran from the pull request and ended before the fix was released. Yours measures something else, and you cannot argue about which interval matters until you know it.
