**AI's hard problems moved to the org chart.**

Kimi K3 topped the community arenas this week and half our feeds declared the frontier race over. We don't think open models are quite at frontier level yet. The more useful observation: the decisions with the longest half-life this week were about people, not model capability.

In Issue 14 of The Shift:

- An essay gave the failure mode a name: AI strategy written under board pressure by people who have not used the tools. The cheap test: how many of your AI commitments were made with hands-on time in the tools?
- Kaiser nurses showed what AI arriving as performance surveillance cost one health system: months of organised resistance.
- Linus Torvalds told critics of AI coding in the Linux kernel to fork it or walk away. Our take: set your team norm on AI-assisted work before the conflict sets one for you, and encode your review logic in review agents, humans on the loop.

Also this week: the measured open-weight gap, a possible FINRA-style AI watchdog, the EU's August 2 transparency deadline, Stack Overflow going quiet, and a reported 1.5TB Mac.

If the hard problems have moved to the org chart, which one on your desk changes a decision this quarter, and which are you letting pass? Reply and tell us.
