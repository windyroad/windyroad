# Issue 14: AI's hard problems moved to the org chart

*The Shift, AI engineering, week ending 2026-07-19*

**From Tom**

Kimi K3 topped the community arenas this week and half my feed declared the frontier race over. I don't think open models are quite at frontier level yet. What struck me more: the decisions with the longest half-life this week were about people, not model capability.

The three stories below sit at three levels of the same org chart. Executives committing to AI they have not used. Nurses organising against how it was deployed on them. A maintainer community fighting over who gets to use it. The week's capability news is still here, in "Also worth noting", with the decisions it carries.

### Item 1: The case against deciding on AI you have not used

**What happened:**
  - A widely shared essay, [AI Mania Is Eviscerating Global Decision-Making](https://ludic.mataroa.blog/blog/ai-mania-is-eviscerating-global-decision-making/), argued that organisational pressure is pushing executives into AI commitments without hands-on experience.
  - The essay's sharpest example: an executive who confessed to never having used ChatGPT or any AI tool, straight after producing an AI-centred technical strategy for an organisation with more than $2 billion in revenue.
  - [Simon Willison amplified it](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/19/ai-mania/) on 19 July, reading it as a warning about poorly informed technology decisions.
  - On the pressure side, The Wall Street Journal profiled [IBM CEO Arvind Krishna as having nowhere to hide from AI](https://www.wsj.com/tech/ibm-ceo-arvind-krishna-has-nowhere-to-hide-from-ai-c9ff290f): a leader squeezed between AI moving too fast and too slow at once.

**Why it matters to your team:** The essay gives the failure mode a name: strategy written under board pressure by people who have not used the tools. One essay and one anecdote is thin evidence, so run the cheap test on your own organisation instead: which of your current AI commitments were made with hands-on time in the tools? We would take an afternoon of hands-on use over a deck of vendor benchmarks.

**The human angle:** A strategy authored by someone who has never opened the tool costs the team that receives it: they can tell, and every later decision inherits the doubt. Put hands-on time on the exec calendar before the next AI commitment.

### Item 2: Kaiser nurses push back on AI used as surveillance

**What happened:**
  - Kaiser Permanente nurses publicly protested that [AI scoring and workplace surveillance are making their jobs and patient care worse](https://localnewsmatters.org/2026/07/15/kaiser-nurses-say-ai-workplace-surveillance-are-making-their-jobs-and-patient-care-worse/). Call time feeds monthly performance scores, and AI rates their empathy and tone of voice.
  - Fast Company reported [nurses protesting the use of AI tools for performance management](https://www.fastcompany.com/91571569/kaiser-nurses-say-ai-is-changing-their-jobs-for-the-worse), claiming it hurts both staff and patients.
  - MedCity News covered [nurses protesting the CEO's appearance at an industry event over AI concerns](https://medcitynews.com/2026/07/kaiser-permanente-ai-nurses/).
  - The organising is AI-specific and months deep: nurses struck against AI for a day in March and picketed against AI last fall.

**Why it matters to your team:** A deployment where the AI is the performance surveillance (scoring calls, rating empathy) can buy months of sustained organised resistance, and that cost lands on any leader whose org scores support, operations, or on-call staff with AI-derived metrics.

**The human angle:** These nurses have software rating their empathy and tone of voice while they do the caring, and that is what staff are reacting to. Our inference, not the record: assistance without the scoring might have landed differently. Separate help from surveillance in your own deployments, and pair any rollout with explicit consent and workload commitments.

### Item 3: Fork it or walk away: Torvalds draws the AI line

**What happened:**
  - Linus Torvalds [rebuked anti-AI stances in the Linux kernel code review process](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/07/linus-torvalds-to-critics-of-ai-coding-in-linux-fork-it-or-just-walk-away/), telling critics of AI coding to fork the project or walk away.
  - The thread that sparked it concerned [Sashiko, an agentic code-review system](https://arstechnica.com/ai/2026/07/linus-torvalds-to-critics-of-ai-coding-in-linux-fork-it-or-just-walk-away/) in the kernel's own review process.
  - The exchange drew wide attention: the [r/LocalLLaMA thread](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1uxbrw4/linus_torvalds_tells_people_to_stop_attacking/) gathered roughly three thousand upvotes.

**Why it matters to your team:** The AI-sceptic versus AI-adopter conflict has reached the most respected engineering community there is, and the kernel fight was specifically about AI in code review. If it surfaces there, expect it in yours. We have argued since Issue 06 that more review will not fix AI slop. Encode your review logic in review agents. Keep humans on the loop setting the rules, not in the loop reading every change. We find AI review picks up more issues than human review, with one non-negotiable: the agent that created the code should not be the agent that reviews it. Agents carry the same confirmation bias we do.

**The human angle:** Tool-shaming in either direction corrodes review culture, and Torvalds leaning in is the counterpoint to the AI-contribution bans we have tracked at Zig, Ladybird, and Godot. Set an explicit team norm on AI-assisted work before the conflict sets one for you: what is allowed, what must be disclosed, and what gets judged purely on the merits.

### Two weeks out: the EU's AI transparency rules land August 2

The EU AI Act's [Article 50 transparency obligations](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/transparency-rules-article-50/) take effect August 2. From that date, users must be told when they are interacting with an AI system, and synthetic content must be machine-readably labelled. If you ship into the EU, inventory the affected features now; if you do not, this is still the template other jurisdictions copy.

One nuance for products already shipping: under the EU's provisionally agreed AI Omnibus package (the amendments that delay parts of the Act), generative systems already on the market would get until December 2 for the machine-readable marking. Until that is formally adopted, August 2 is the date to plan against.

### Also worth noting

**Kimi K3 and the measured gap.** Moonshot's Kimi K3 [topped community arena rankings ahead of closed frontier models](https://www.reddit.com/r/LocalLLaMA/comments/1uydii0/kimi_k3_beats_claude_fable_and_gpt_56_sol_in/). CNBC reported [the model rivals OpenAI's and Anthropic's](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/17/moonshot-ai-kimi-k3-model-openai-anthropic-china.html), and the New York Times reported [the release threatening America's AI lead](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/17/business/china-ai-moonshot-kimi.html). The more useful number came from the UK AI Safety Institute, which [measured leading open-weight models at 4 to 7 months behind the frontier on cyber tasks](https://www.aisi.gov.uk/blog/how-far-behind-the-frontier-are-leading-open-weight-models-on-cyber), down from 6 to 10 months through 2025. That measurement covers one domain and predates K3, and months behind is not parity (we argued last issue that raw model rank is a poor guide). Still, a closing gap an independent institute measures in months is bargaining power at your next closed-vendor renewal.

**The US is weighing a FINRA-style AI watchdog.** Bloomberg reported [the US considering a self-regulatory watchdog to vet top AI models](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-07-17/us-considers-creating-finra-like-watchdog-to-vet-top-ai-models), modelled on FINRA, the body that self-regulates US securities brokers. It is consideration, not a rule yet. But if standing model vetting hardens, the evaluation records and model inventory you keep become compliance assets: the same records serve engineering today and compliance later.

**Vendors are writing your AI scorecard.** OpenAI published [guidance on managing AI investments in the agentic era](https://openai.com/index/managing-ai-investments-in-agentic-era/) and [a scorecard for the AI age](https://openai.com/index/a-scorecard-for-the-ai-age/), while The AI Daily Brief framed [the new enterprise battle over who owns the model](https://aidailybrief.ai/e/2026-07-16). Read vendor playbooks as marketing that still contains useful material. Set your own measures before a vendor's scorecard becomes your default: cost per completed task, adoption against a named workflow, and evaluation pass-rates on your own tasks. Put them on one page the exec team reviews quarterly.

**Stack Overflow is going quiet.** A [Stack Exchange data-explorer graph](https://data.stackexchange.com/stackoverflow/query/1953768#graph) showing new-question volume collapsing to early-2009 levels was widely shared this week. The answer archive is still there, but it stops covering new tools and versions as new questions dry up. Pick one concrete move this quarter: audit where your juniors get answers today, or fund the internal Q&A and docs practice that replaces what they lost.

**Fable 5 is staying.** Simon Willison [reported that Anthropic reversed its plan to remove Claude Fable 5 from subscription plans](https://simonwillison.net/2026/Jul/18/claude-make-fable-5-permanent/); it stays on Max and Team Premium plans at half usage limits. Model-availability churn cut in customers' favour this time, which does not change the lesson we drew before: get model-continuity commitments into your vendor contract.

**A 1.5TB Mac is reportedly coming.** Tom's Hardware relayed [a report that Apple's M7 Ultra targets up to 1.5TB of unified memory](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/semiconductors/apples-rumored-m7-ultra-targets-1-5tb-of-memory-and-blackwell-class-ai), with AI performance in the class of Nvidia's Blackwell data-centre chips. Not yet shipped, and rumours slip, so the move is to wait. Keep any inference-hosting or hardware-refresh decision with a horizon past 2027 flexible until this confirms or dies, because a workstation that holds a near-frontier open-weight model in memory changes the on-premises maths.
