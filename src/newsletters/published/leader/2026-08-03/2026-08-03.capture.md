## Item 7: GCC steering committee published an AI contribution policy

- **Theme role**: deep item 1 (theme-defining; re-adjudicated after a factual correction, see CORRECTION below)
- **Outcome**: Agree, with adjust; then re-adjudicated to "Run it as the wrong rule" after correction
- **Original take presented**: The refusals were easy to dismiss as purist. A compiler project writing rules for how AI-assisted contributions are accepted is a usable template, and setting your own stance now beats having a maintainer, auditor or customer set it for you.
- **Adjust text** (given twice, unprompted, on both the Item 5 and Item 7 questions):

  > Agree and I'd be interested to dive deeper into understanding their policy to see if it's good or bad, but that fact that it's not a Blanket ban is a good step forward. If however they require human review of AI generated code, rather than human review of the AI review process, then I don't think it's a good policy for the reasons I've outlined before

  > I agree, and it's good to see that it's Not a blanket ban. But if it requires human review with the code then it's a poor policy. Instead it needs to be human review of the AI review process for the reasons I've outlined before.

- **Drafter direction**: this is the strongest POV in the capture and the source of the edition's through-line. Preserve the load-bearing construction verbatim or near-verbatim: **human review of the AI review process, not human review of the AI-generated code**. This aligns with the codified P101 editorial stance in `three-lens-filter.md`.

- **CORRECTION (applied before drafting, 2026-08-02)**: the premise Tom's capture was given rested on was FALSE and was supplied by the drafter. The question presented GCC's policy as "the first to publish a governing policy rather than a blanket refusal", and Tom agreed twice on that basis ("it's good to see that it's Not a blanket ban"). Verification of the actual policy at `https://lwn.net/Articles/1086041/` (Jul 29) shows it IS a blanket ban: GCC declines any "legally significant contributions which include LLM-generated content or are derived from LLM-generated content", threshold around 15 lines of code and/or text per GNU maintainer guidelines, with a narrow exception for LLM-generated test cases. The policy DOES explicitly permit LLM use for research, analysis, bug discovery and reporting, and patch review, provided no output reaches the contribution. So GCC permits AI in the review and requires humans in the artifact, which is close to the inverse of Tom's test.
- **Re-adjudication**: the correction was surfaced to Tom with the verbatim policy text before drafting. He selected **"Run it as the wrong rule"**. Item 7 therefore flips from qualified praise to criticism and becomes the lead deep item: GCC is the edition's primary example of writing the wrong rule, because it puts humans on the artifact by prohibition while letting AI do the reviewing. Tom's underlying POV is unchanged and still governs; only the fact pattern flipped.
- **Process note (P032 recurrence)**: this is a verify-before-asserting failure introduced by the drafter at capture time, not by a source. The presented "Our take" asserted the content of a policy that had not been read. Capture questions that characterise the substance of a named artifact should read the artifact first.
